# V4.4 Readium Stability Pass II

This pass stays focused on reliability and reading feel rather than adding a new study module.

1. The Readium word-tap bridge is installed immediately when a resource becomes current. It is no longer coupled to the 700 ms progress-save debounce.
2. Word-tap installation retries briefly while a newly loaded chapter finishes attaching its WebView.
3. EPUB word taps again trigger pronunciation automatically, matching Lirelia's legacy reader behavior.
4. A long-press text selection is protected from the WebView's trailing click event, so releasing a selection no longer opens the word-translation dock by accident.
5. Selection tracking remains live while the floating toolbar is visible, with lower polling pressure than V4.3.
6. Scroll-mode previous/next now crosses resource/chapter boundaries when the current document has reached its edge.
7. Auto-scroll uses the active scrolling element and can continue into the next resource.
8. Annotation snapshots, decoration preparation, note/highlight writes and legacy EPUB search migration work are kept off the Compose/UI thread.
9. Readium bookmark position generation used by legacy migration also runs on IO.
10. Main and Readium reader activities deterministically close DictionaryRepository and LearningStore handles after their UI scopes are disposed.
11. One-shot LearningStore use during book deletion is closed deterministically as well.
12. DictionaryRepository exposes an idempotent close() and avoids SQLite validation on the normal WikDict startup path when no interrupted-swap backup exists.
13. The underlying dictionary result no longer caps meanings at 10/12/16 entries. Full dictionary views can now truly show every stored meaning; quick cards intentionally remain concise.
14. Holding a hardware volume key no longer repeats page turns in either the Readium reader or the legacy reader.
15. Android back dismisses transient Readium translation/selection/chrome layers before leaving the book.

Ollama remains removed. Only upgrade cleanup for the two historical Ollama preference keys remains.
